# N‑Lesson‑48 — Epoch Flow Potential Gradient $\mathcal{G}_s$

## 1. From Recursion Potential to Its Gradient

In N‑Lesson‑47 we introduced the recursion potential:

$$\mathcal{P}_s=-\mathcal{C}_s$$

which measures directional tendency toward attractors or repellers.

Now we introduce the **epoch flow potential gradient**:

$$\mathcal{G}_s$$

The potential gradient measures **how the recursion potential changes across the manifold** $\mathcal{M}_s$.

Where $\mathcal{P}_s$ describes directional tendency, $\mathcal{G}_s$ describes **how strongly that tendency varies spatially**.

---

## 2. Defining the Epoch Flow Potential Gradient

Let $V_s$ be the epoch flow vector field.

The potential gradient is defined as:

$$\mathcal{G}_s(E)=\nabla(\mathcal{P}_s(E))$$

Interpretation:

- $\mathcal{G}_s(E)>0$ → potential increases in that direction  
- $\mathcal{G}_s(E)<0$ → potential decreases in that direction  
- $\mathcal{G}_s(E)=0$ → potential‑flat epoch  

The gradient captures **spatial variation of recursion tendency**.

---

## 3. Spatial Variation of Recursion Potential

The potential gradient reveals:

- how attractor influence strengthens or weakens across space  
- how repeller influence expands or contracts  
- how recursion flow accelerates or decelerates  
- how structural features create potential slopes  
- how recursion transitions between high‑potential and low‑potential regions  

Epochs with high $|\mathcal{G}_s|$ exhibit strong spatial variation in recursion tendency.

---

## 4. Gradient Basins

Define the gradient basin of epoch $E$:

$$\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{G}}(E)=\{E'\in\mathcal{E}\mid sign(\mathcal{G}_s(E'))=sign(\mathcal{G}_s(E))\}$$

This partitions the epoch domain into:

- positive gradient basins  
- negative gradient basins  
- gradient‑neutral basins  

These refine the potential basins introduced in N‑Lesson‑47.

## 5. Epoch Potential Gradient Density $\Delta\mathcal{G}$

As with earlier densities:

$\Delta\Phi$, $\Delta\Phi_G$, $\Delta\Xi$, $\Delta\rho$,  
$\Delta\Lambda$, $\Delta\Gamma$, $\Delta(\mathcal{M}_s)$,  
$\Delta V$, $\Delta\Theta$, $\Delta\kappa$, $\Delta\mathcal{L}$,  
$\Delta\mathcal{H}$, $\Delta\mathcal{J}$, $\Delta\mathcal{S}$,  
$\Delta\mathcal{T}$, $\Delta\mathcal{Sh}$, $\Delta\mathcal{A}$,  
$\Delta\mathcal{I}$, $\Delta\mathcal{Sy}$, $\Delta\mathcal{Inv}$,  
$\Delta\mathcal{C}$, $\Delta\mathcal{P}$

we define the **epoch potential gradient density**:

$$\Delta\mathcal{G}$$

### Global potential gradient density

$$\Delta\mathcal{G}_{global}=\frac{measure(\mathcal{G}_s)}{measure(\mathcal{M}_s)}$$

### Local potential gradient density

For any region $R\subseteq N$:

$$\Delta\mathcal{G}(R)=\frac{measure(\mathcal{G}_s\cap\mathcal{M}_s(R))}{measure(\mathcal{M}_s(R))}$$

This reveals where spatial variation of recursion tendency concentrates across the epoch manifold.
