# N‑Lesson‑0016 — Structural Flows and Multi-Step Optimization
Ambient Flow Fields and Discrete Structural Evolution

---

## 1. Purpose

Lessons 0011–0015 established:

• ambient derivatives (gradient, Jacobian, Hessian),  
• ambient Laplacian and divergence,  
• discrete descent dynamics on P.

Lesson 0016 introduces **structural flows**:

• ambient flow fields in ℓ²,  
• discrete flow trajectories in P,  
• multi-step optimisation paths,  
• stability and monotonicity conditions for structural evolution.

All flows on P are discrete; ambient flows provide guidance.

---

## 2. Ambient Flow Fields

Let Φ : U → ℝ be differentiable on an open neighbourhood U ⊆ ℓ² containing ψ(P).

Define the **ambient flow field**:

    X(p) := −∇Φ(ψ(p)) ∈ ℓ².

This is the ambient analogue of a gradient flow direction.

Interpretation:

• X(p) points in the direction of steepest ambient decrease of Φ.  
• X(p) is not necessarily tangent to ψ(P).  
• X(p) may not correspond to any structural path in P.

---

## 3. Structural Flow Graph

Let 𝒩(p) ⊆ P be the finite adjacency neighbourhood of p under the unit-step
relation of Lesson 0001.

Define the **structural flow graph**:

    p → q    whenever q ∈ 𝒩(p).

This graph encodes all admissible one-step structural transitions.

---

## 4. Ambient Projection onto Structural Moves

For each neighbour q ∈ 𝒩(p), define the displacement:

    δ(p, q) = ψ(q) − ψ(p).

Project the ambient flow direction X(p) onto each structural displacement:

    P(p, q) := ⟨X(p), δ(p, q)⟩.

Interpretation:

• P(p, q) < 0 → ambient flow points toward q.  
• P(p, q) > 0 → ambient flow points away from q.  
• P(p, q) = 0 → ambient flow orthogonal to δ(p, q).

This projection is the ambient analogue of directional flow.

---

## 5. Structural Flow Step

Define the **structural flow step**:

    p_{n+1} ∈ argmin_{q ∈ 𝒩(p_n)} P(p_n, q).

This selects the neighbour most aligned with the ambient flow direction.

Because P(p_n, q) is an ambient estimate, the step may not strictly decrease F.

---

## 6. Monotone Structural Flow

To ensure monotonic decrease of F, restrict to neighbours that decrease F:

    D(p) := { q ∈ 𝒩(p) : F(q) ≤ F(p) }.

Define the **monotone structural flow step**:

    p_{n+1} ∈ argmin_{q ∈ D(p_n)} P(p_n, q).

This ensures:

• structural validity (q ∈ 𝒩(p_n)),  
• monotonicity (F(q) ≤ F(p_n)),  
• ambient alignment (P minimal among monotone neighbours).

---

## 7. Multi-Step Structural Flow Trajectories

A **structural flow trajectory** is a sequence:

    p_0 → p_1 → p_2 → ⋯

generated by repeated application of the monotone structural flow rule.

Properties:

• Trajectories remain in P.  
• Trajectories follow adjacency edges.  
• Trajectories are guided by ambient flow but constrained by structural validity.

---

## 8. Second-Order Flow Guidance

If Φ is twice Fréchet differentiable, the Hessian operator T_H(p) provides
second-order curvature information.

Define:

    Q(p, q) := HF(p)[δ(p, q), δ(p, q)]
             = ⟨T_H(p) δ(p, q), δ(p, q)⟩.

A **second-order flow rule** prefers neighbours satisfying:

• P(p, q) < 0 (ambient alignment),  
• Q(p, q) small or negative (favourable curvature),  
• F(q) ≤ F(p) (monotonicity).

This yields a curvature-aware structural flow.

---

## 9. Laplacian-Based Flow Smoothing

The discrete structural Laplacian from Lesson 0014:

    Δ_struct F(p)
      = Σ_{q ∈ 𝒩(p)} (F(q) − F(p))

measures local variation of F around p.

Define the **Laplacian-smoothed flow direction**:

    S(p) := −Δ_struct F(p).

Interpretation:

• S(p) < 0 → local neighbourhood suggests decreasing F.  
• S(p) > 0 → local neighbourhood suggests increasing F.

A **smoothed structural flow step** is:

    p_{n+1} ∈ argmin_{q ∈ 𝒩(p_n)} ( P(p_n, q),  S(p_n) ).

This combines ambient flow with neighbourhood smoothing.

## 10. Examples

### Example 1 — Quadratic Magnitude Functional

Let:

    Φ(x) = ‖x‖₂²,
    F(p) = ‖ψ(p)‖₂².

Then:

    X(p) = −2ψ(p),
    P(p, q) = −2⟨ψ(p), δ(p, q)⟩,
    Q(p, q) = 2‖δ(p, q)‖₂².

Flow moves toward neighbours whose embeddings ψ(q) lie closer to the origin.

---

### Example 2 — Weighted Quadratic Functional

Let:

    Φ(x) = Σ w_{ij} x_{ij}²,

with w ∈ ℓ∞. Then:

    X(p)_{ij} = −2 w_{ij} ψ(p)_{ij},
    T_H(p) u = (2 w_{ij} u_{ij})_{i,j}.

Flow prefers neighbours that reduce weighted coordinates.

---

### Example 3 — Bounded Linear Functional

Let Φ : ℓ² → ℝ be bounded linear.  
By Riesz, Φ(x) = ⟨g, x⟩ for a unique g ∈ ℓ².

Then:

    X(p) = −g,
    P(p, q) = −⟨g, δ(p, q)⟩,
    Q(p, q) = 0.

Flow is purely first-order.

---

## 11. Interpretation

Ambient flow:

• provides a direction in ℓ²,  
• depends on the chosen extension Φ,  
• is not itself a structural path.

Structural flow:

• uses adjacency in P,  
• is always well-defined,  
• moves only to valid partitions,  
• uses ambient information as guidance.

Multi-step structural flow:

• produces discrete trajectories,  
• may converge to local minima,  
• may exhibit plateaus or cycles depending on neighbourhood geometry.
