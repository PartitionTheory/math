## 2. Definition of Recursive Graph Multiplication (×G)

Let N be a structural domain, and let p and q be partition indices.

Definition:

Gₛ(p) ×G Gₛ(q) = ΩG^{ΔG(q)}(Gₛ(p))

Where:
- Gₛ(p) is the adjacency graph generated from p,
- ΔG(q) is the graph‑level density of q,
- ΩG is the graph‑level successor operator,
- ΩG^{ΔG(q)}(Gₛ(p)) means applying ΩG repeatedly ΔG(q) times.

In words:
×G multiplies adjacency graphs by applying graph‑level successor motion a number of times equal 
to the density of the second graph.

## 3. Properties of ×G

### 3.1 Non‑Arithmetic
×G does not compute numeric graph products.  
It computes structural graph displacement.

### 3.2 Density‑Driven
ΔG determines the number of successor steps.

### 3.3 Ordering‑Driven
ΩG determines the successor direction.

### 3.4 Graph‑Level
Gₛ determines the graph structure being displaced.

### 3.5 Domain‑Dependent
×G depends entirely on the N‑domain.

### 3.6 Reversible
×G has an inverse operator using ΩG⁻¹.
