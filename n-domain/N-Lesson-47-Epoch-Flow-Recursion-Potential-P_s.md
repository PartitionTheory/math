# N‑Lesson‑47 — Epoch Flow Recursion Potential $\mathcal{P}_s$

## 1. From Conservation to Potential

In N‑Lesson‑46 we introduced the conservation operator:

$$\mathcal{C}_s=\nabla\cdot(V_s)$$

which measures global preservation of recursion quantities.

Now we introduce the **epoch flow recursion potential**:

$$\mathcal{P}_s$$

The recursion potential measures **how strongly recursion flow is attracted or repelled** by structural features of the manifold $\mathcal{M}_s$.

Where conservation describes global balance, recursion potential describes **directional tendency**.

---

## 2. Defining the Epoch Flow Recursion Potential

Let $V_s$ be the epoch flow vector field.

The recursion potential is defined as the negative divergence of the flow:

$$\mathcal{P}_s(E)=-\mathcal{C}_s(E)$$

Interpretation:

- $\mathcal{P}_s(E)>0$ → recursion tends inward (attractor‑seeking)  
- $\mathcal{P}_s(E)<0$ → recursion tends outward (repeller‑seeking)  
- $\mathcal{P}_s(E)=0$ → potential‑neutral epoch  

The recursion potential captures **directional tendency of recursion flow**.

---

## 3. Attractor and Repeller Behavior

The recursion potential reveals:

- how recursion moves toward attractors  
- how recursion escapes repellers  
- how flow geometry channels recursion  
- how structural features influence recursion direction  
- how recursion transitions between attraction and repulsion regimes  

Epochs with high $|\mathcal{P}_s|$ exhibit strong directional tendencies.

## 4. Potential Basins

Define the potential basin of epoch $E$:

$$\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{P}}(E)=\{E'\in\mathcal{E}\mid sign(\mathcal{P}_s(E'))=sign(\mathcal{P}_s(E))\}$$

This partitions the epoch domain into:

- positive potential basins (attractor‑seeking)  
- negative potential basins (repeller‑seeking)  
- potential‑neutral basins  

These refine the conservation basins introduced in N‑Lesson‑46.
