## Summary of the Work

The document introduces the **PMachine**, also called the **Partition Machine**, as an entry-level lesson in Partition Theory.

The lesson develops the PMachine through a human-readable sequence:

- left-bounded tape;
- finite partition input;
- operational memory;
- clock;
- N and N−1 presentations;
- visual grid displays;
- pattern–clock–information correspondence;
- teacher/grid-paper aids;
- clock-state recovery;
- the visual tape paradox;
- formal mathematical definition;
- theorem-level formulation.

The completed work presents the PMachine as a finite ordered product-memory mechanism in which:

- the partition input determines the operational memory size;
- the product of the input groups determines the clock;
- ordered block structure determines internal numerical state;
- displayed clock values can be restored and decoded by subtraction and remainder passage;
- teaching stacks are visual aids, not computational requirements;
- N and N−1 are distinct presentations of the same instructional machine context;
- visual sameness of tape notation does not alone determine machine state.

## Teaching Displays and Computation

A central distinction in the work is that the visible stacks and colour-coded grids are teaching aids.

They are not presented as a recommendation that the PMachine should compute by building an entire stack.

The strength of the PMachine is that the input itself determines the operational memory size. Given a clock position, the machine state can be recovered directly by restoring the clock value and passing remainders through the ordered internal block values.

The colour displays are included because they make the structure visible to human learners.

---

## N and N−1

During the creation of the document, EF Smyth taught CO the beginner PMachine distinction between **N** and **N−1**.

In this lesson:

- **N** is the whole teaching presentation.
- **N−1** is the reduced teaching presentation obtained by removing the first-position `f/a` cells from each group while retaining the clock axis.
- `s` remains the formal machine value.
- `vs` is introduced only as a teacher/grid-paper visual aid.

The N versus N−1 distinction is used to clarify the visual tape paradox: identical or visually similar inscriptions may have different machine meanings unless their governing presentation is specified.
